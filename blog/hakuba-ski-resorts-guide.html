Hakuba Ski Resorts Guide (Which One is Best?)

白馬スキー場ガイド
March 2026 · 7 min read
Add hero image here

Trying to choose a ski resort in Hakuba is a bit like looking at a dessert menu when you're already full. Everything looks good, and you're slightly overwhelmed.

Because here's the thing. Hakuba isn't one resort.

It's ten.

All sitting in the same valley, each one a little different. Some feel big and dramatic, others are cruisy and tree lined, like you've wandered into a snow globe.

So which one is actually the best? Short answer. It depends on you. Long answer. Here's how it really breaks down.

First, what is Hakuba?

Hakuba is a collection of ski resorts in Nagano, set in the Japanese Alps.

There are more than 200 runs spread across different mountains, all connected by buses or short drives. It's less one mega resort and more choosing your day based on the conditions.

Which is why where you ski, and where you stay, matters more than people think.

The main Hakuba ski resorts

Happo One

If Hakuba had a main character, this would be it.

Happo One is the biggest and most well known resort. Long runs, steeper sections, and a proper alpine feel. It's where you go when you want to feel like you're actually skiing.

  • Intermediate to advanced riders
  • Long runs and varied terrain
  • People who do not mind a bit of energy and crowds

Not ideal for complete beginners.

Tsugaike

Tsugaike is just easy. In the best way.

Wide runs, forgiving slopes, and heaps of space. It's the kind of place where you can relax into your day instead of fighting for it.

There is even a Burger King halfway up the mountain, which still feels random every time. There is also a solid terrain park, so it is not just for beginners.

  • Beginners and early intermediates
  • Park laps and progression
  • Powder days without the chaos
  • Anyone who just wants a good day

Relaxed, open, and quietly one of the best all rounders. Note: parking on weekends is about 1000 yen.

Iwatake

Iwatake feels different straight away.

It is not about ticking off runs. It is more about the whole day. The views are unreal, the pace is slower, and there are actually places you want to stop.

At the top there is a bakery that is worth it alone. Good coffee, something warm, and one of those views that makes you pause for a second.

They have also added a new gondola which makes getting up there much smoother.

  • Mixed groups
  • Scenic cruising
  • People who like a bit of lifestyle with their skiing

Less rush, more stay a while.

Hakuba 47 and Goryu

These two are connected and you feel it straight away.

Good parks, well groomed runs, and a nice mix of terrain. If you are trying to improve, this is a solid place to spend time.

On your way home, stop by Sweet Shot Hakuba. Easily some of the best sweet treats in town.

  • Freestyle riders
  • Intermediates wanting to improve
  • People who like consistency

Cortina

When it snows, everyone quietly heads to Cortina.

It is known for deep powder and tree runs. The kind of days where you are not really skiing, you are just floating and hoping your legs hold up.

  • Powder days
  • Advanced riders
  • Anyone chasing proper Japow

Just a heads up. Not the place for beginners, especially on storm days.

Norikura

Norikura flies under the radar, which is exactly why it is so good.

It connects to Cortina but feels calmer and less busy. You can cruise the groomers or step things up.

If you are looking at backcountry, this is one of the more accessible places to start. Gate 11 gives you access, but it is not something to take lightly. You will need avalanche gear and to know what you are doing. There are places in town where you can hire gear if needed.

There is also a seasonal fee for Gate 11 access, around 2500 yen.

  • Backcountry riders
  • Powder without the crowds
  • A mix of mellow and more advanced terrain

Sanosaka

Then there is Sanosaka.

Right by a lake, it feels completely different. Calmer, slower, almost surreal. On a clear day you are skiing with water beside you, which feels slightly wrong but in a good way.

It is not about size. It is about the feeling.

And do not rush off after. Stop by Cake AO, or go one better and jump into the Hakuba Zekkei Sauna right on the water. Coffee, something sweet, a sauna, frozen lake.

Quick reference

  • Want big terrain — Happo One
  • Want easy and reliable — Tsugaike
  • Want views and a slower pace — Iwatake
  • Want park laps — 47, Goryu or Tsugaike
  • Want powder — Cortina
  • Want backcountry — Norikura
  • Want something different — Sanosaka

The best part about Hakuba is you do not have to commit. You just pick based on the day. Weather, snow, how your legs are feeling. It changes everything.

Where you stay matters more than you think

Because everything is spread out, your base can shape your whole trip.

Stay somewhere busy and it feels busy. Stay somewhere quieter and the whole experience shifts.

That is why areas like Ochikura are such a good spot. You are right between Tsugaike and Iwatake, but without the noise.

Powder Jungle sits tucked in the trees in Ochikura. Close to everything, but just far enough away to feel calm when you get back.

Final thoughts

Hakuba does not really work like picking one resort and sticking to it. It is more fluid than that.

One day you are chasing powder. Next day you are cruising in the sun. Somewhere in between, you stop caring which mountain you are on.

It is just a good day.

My personal favourite

If I had to pick one, it is Tsugaike.

No matter what the forecast says, you know you are going to have a good time. There is so much terrain, and it sits high enough that even when it is average down low, you can usually find something better up top.

It is reliable but never boring. Easy, but still fun.

It delivers.

And honestly — who does not want Burger King on the mountain?
